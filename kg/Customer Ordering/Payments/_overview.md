# Payments — Overview

## What is this? (for everyone)

How a customer pays, and what happens to that money afterwards.

A customer can pay **cash** when the order arrives, from their **8Orders wallet**, or **online** by
card or e-wallet. Which of those they are offered depends on the country they are ordering in — not
every method exists everywhere.

The part most people underestimate is what happens after the payment. One order's money is split
several ways: the restaurant is owed for the food, 8Orders keeps its commission, the driver either owes
8Orders the cash they collected or is owed their delivery earnings, and any discount has to be carried
by someone. Every one of those becomes an accounting entry, and at the end of the day the entries are
sent to 8Orders' accounting system. That whole journey is written up separately, step by step, in
[[Money-Path.business|The Money Path]].

Two related things that sound like customer payment methods but are not:

- **Fawry** here is how a **driver hands in the cash they collected**, not a way for a customer to pay.
- **The customer wallet balance** is not a stored number. It is the sum of every wallet entry ever
  written for that customer, so a wrong entry changes history rather than just the balance.

## Business Flow (plain language)

1. At checkout the customer is shown the methods allowed where they are ordering.
2. If they pay online, they are taken to the payment provider's page and sent back afterwards. 8Orders
   only believes the payment once the provider confirms it directly — and if that confirmation looks
   tampered with, 8Orders asks the provider again rather than trusting the message.
3. If they pay by wallet, the amount is written as a wallet entry.
4. If they pay cash, nothing moves until the driver collects it, at which point the driver owes 8Orders
   that cash.
5. When the order completes, the money is split into accounting entries for the restaurant, 8Orders'
   commission, and the driver.
6. Drivers hand in collected cash separately; 8Orders records both what the driver said they paid and
   what the collection provider confirms.
7. Periodically all of it is posted to the accounting system's ledger.

## Key Concepts

- **Payment method** — cash, wallet, online card, Orange Money, or e-wallet. Availability is per
  country.
- **Wallet** — a customer's stored balance, held as a list of entries rather than a single figure.
- **Recharge reason** — when someone at 8Orders credits a customer's wallet by hand, they must pick a
  reason from a fixed list, so the entry is explainable later.
- **Settlement entry** — one accounting line generated by an order (restaurant owed, commission,
  driver, discount contribution).
- **Driver remittance** — a driver paying in the cash they collected, through Fawry.
- **Treasury receipt** — the accounting-system record that online money actually arrived.

## What a business reader should know about trust here

The customer-facing part of payments is small and correctly locked down: both wallet screens can only
ever show the signed-in customer's own balance and history — the account is taken from the login, not
from the request.

The risk in payments is not access, it is **reconciliation**. The money path has several recorded
issues of that kind, documented in the technical notes rather than here: accounting entries that can be
posted against a missing account, one amount stored as text on one side of the driver-remittance rail
and as a number on the other, and an online-payment record that tracks its state in two separate
true/false flags rather than one status — so "not started" and "finished unsuccessfully" can look alike
to code that checks only one of them.
